Reading good topology

A sculpt or scan has millions of triangles and no structure. Retopology rebuilds it as a light mesh whose edges are placed on purpose. This module is about what "on purpose" means: edge loops, poles, and why their placement decides whether a model animates, unwraps and bakes well.

1 · Edge loops and poles

Loops run straight through regular vertices and stop at poles

In a quad mesh most vertices have 4 edges. At such a vertex an edge has exactly one partner straight across, so you can keep walking "straight ahead". That chain is an edge loop, and it is what artists select, slide, cut and animate along.

A vertex with 3 or 5 edges is a pole (also called an irregular vertex or singularity). There is no straight-across edge there, so loops end or change course. Poles let edge flow turn corners and change density, so they are not mistakes. Where they sit is the craft.

2 · Why loops matter for animation

A joint bends only as well as the loops around it

When a character is rigged, each vertex follows one or more bones, blended by weights. Edges stay straight between vertices, so a joint can only curve where there are vertices to curve. Both strips below use the same bones and the same smooth weights. The only difference is whether edge loops sit around the joint. In 3D each vertical line would be a ring of edges around the limb.

3 · Quads in a triangle world

Game engines draw triangles, so why do artists want quads?

Every quad becomes two triangles on export, and the engine never sees a quad. Quads still matter because of what happens before export:

  • Editing. Loops and rings can be selected, slid and cut only on quads. Triangles break the chain.
  • Deformation. Loops give joints, mouths and eyelids rings of vertices to bend with, as above.
  • UVs. Grid-like quads unwrap into straight, evenly spaced UV lines, which keeps textures undistorted and packing efficient.
  • Subdivision and LODs. Quads subdivide and reduce predictably, while triangles create pinches.
  • Baking and shading. Even, well-shaped faces give clean normal bakes. Long, thin triangles cause shading artefacts.

Triangulation is the last step, and with quads you control which diagonal each quad gets.

4 · A rule you can't break

Closed shapes need poles, and the count is fixed by the shape

Give each vertex a score of 4 − (number of edges): a 3-pole scores +1, a 5-pole −1 and a regular vertex 0. On any closed all-quad mesh the scores add up to a number fixed by the number of holes through the shape. It follows from Euler's formula (V − E + F), which Module 1 covers, and Module 4 shows the same rule for direction fields.

ShapeHoles through itSum of scoresWhat that means
Sphere, head, closed body0+8At least eight 3-poles, as on a subdivided cube. Extra poles must come in balancing pairs, for example ten 3-poles and two 5-poles.
Torus, ring, donut10Can be made with no poles at all.
Two-hole shape (pretzel half, two-handled mug)2−8Needs a net of eight extra 5-poles.

So the question is never whether there are poles, only where they go. Artists put them where edge flow has to turn or change density (mouth corners, eye corners, the shoulder, the base of fingers) and keep them off areas that bend hard or catch highlights. Vertices with 6 or more edges are usually avoided. Mobius's direction field (Module 4) decides pole placement automatically, and your strokes and regions steer it.

6 · Hands-on

Remesh one of your sculpts with two existing tools

Use one model you know well, ideally a head or a character with a face. Work on a copy (Shift+D in Blender), because QuadriFlow replaces the mesh in place.

A · Blender's QuadriFlow

  1. Select the copy in Object Mode.
  2. Open Properties → Object Data (green triangle tab) and find the Remesh panel, or press F3 and search QuadriFlow Remesh.
  3. Choose the Quad mode and click QuadriFlow Remesh.
  4. Set a face count (try 3,000), and try it with and without Use Mesh Symmetry and Preserve Sharp.

QuadriFlow needs a clean, closed (manifold) mesh and reports an error otherwise. Module 1 explains why. Option names can differ slightly between Blender versions.

B · Instant Meshes

  1. Download the Windows build linked from github.com/wjakob/instant-meshes, and export your model from Blender as OBJ.
  2. Click Open mesh, then set a target size close to the QuadriFlow run.
  3. Click the first blue button to solve the orientation field. The little crosses are a 4-RoSy field, the subject of Module 4.
  4. Try a brush tool (the first icon in a "Tool" row) to comb directions on the face, solve again and watch the crosses follow.
  5. Click the second blue button to solve the position field, then Export mesh and import it into Blender.

Instant Meshes is the method Mobius's meshing step builds on (design D6). Its brushes are a primitive form of Mobius's guidance strokes.

Decision 001 · output mode

What should Mobius produce by default: only quads, or mostly quads?

Mobius builds all-quad meshes by making a coarser mesh and splitting every face into quads. That guarantees pure quads, but every leftover triangle becomes a 3-pole and every pentagon a 5-pole, and it gives slightly less local control over face count.
